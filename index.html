the mixer macOS forgot

Windows has had a per-app volume mixer for twenty years. Your Mac has one now.

Per-app volume & mute

Each app gets its own slider and mute button, relative to the master. Relaunch the app and it comes back at 100%.

Pause the right thing

Play/pause Spotify, Music, TV, QuickTime or VLC directly — even Spotify playing on your office speakers — instead of guessing what the ⏯ key will hit.

Why "System Audio Recording" for a volume app?

macOS has no per-app volume API. The only driverless way to control one app's sound is to tap it: clipvolume asks Core Audio to mute that app at the speakers and hands the audio back to clipvolume, which plays it at the volume you chose. Apple files that capability under System Audio Recording. Apps you haven't touched are never routed through clipvolume, nothing is written to disk, and the app makes no network connections — the source is right there.

questions

The short answers

Can it pause a YouTube tab?
It can mute one instantly. Browsers don't expose play/pause to other apps, so a tab can't be paused from outside — Spotify, Music, TV, QuickTime Player and VLC can.
How does it know Spotify is playing on my office speakers?
When Spotify is playing via Spotify Connect it makes no sound on your Mac, so it wouldn't appear in a normal mixer. clipvolume asks the Spotify app for its state and shows it as a row anyway — Playing on another device — with a pause button that works.
What happens to my per-app settings?
They last as long as the app does. Quit Spotify and relaunch it and it's back at 100%, unmuted. Muted apps keep their row while they're running, even when they go quiet, so you can always unmute.
Does it add latency?
Only for apps you've turned down or muted, and only a few milliseconds — the audio goes through one extra Core Audio buffer. Apps at 100% are never touched.
Why isn't it on the Mac App Store?
Process taps don't work reliably inside the App Store sandbox. So it ships the way SoundSource and Loopback do: direct download, signed with a Developer ID, notarized by Apple, no security warning.
Which macOS versions?
macOS 14.4 (Sonoma) or later — that's when Apple gave process taps their own permission. Apple silicon and Intel.
